The article compares interstellar exploration with realistic science fiction. It argues that even the most advanced missions inside the solar system need no advances in basic science, and that robotic flyby missions to the nearest stars with nanoprobes are feasible with known science. Anything beyond requires advances we do not know how to achieve, or whether they are even possible. The slowest missions, such as generation ships or hibernation, depend more on space medicine and biology than on propulsion. The fastest allowed by relativity are relativistic missions, which exploit time contraction.
