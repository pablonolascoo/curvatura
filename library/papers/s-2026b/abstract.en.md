In this paper, we investigate holographic dark energy (HDE) motivated energy density profiles as viable matter sources for the construction of traversable wormholes in the framework of $f(R,T)$ gravity. Wormhole solutions are developed using entropy-corrected HDE models based on the Bekenstein--Hawking, Rényi, and Moradpour formalisms. We derive the corresponding shape functions and analyze the influence of the model parameters on the flare-out conditions, obtaining the parameter bounds required for traversability. The null energy condition is examined under these constraints and is found to be violated near the wormhole throat, thereby providing the exotic matter content necessary to sustain the geometry. We further evaluate the total gravitational energy and the complexity factor, showing that the internal structure of the wormhole spacetime is highly sensitive to the underlying entropy prescription. The regularity of the solutions is tested through curvature invariants, confirming the absence of curvature singularities and the asymptotic decay of the Kretschmann scalar. In addition, the generalized Tolman--Oppenheimer--Volkoff equation is used to study hydrostatic equilibrium, where the inward hydrostatic force is balanced by the anisotropic force and the extra force induced by the matter--geometry coupling. Finally, scalar and electromagnetic quasinormal modes are computed using the third-order WKB approximation. The negative imaginary parts of all obtained frequencies indicate damped oscillations and hence perturbative stability of the wormhole configurations. Our results show that entropy-corrected HDE models, particularly the Rényi and Moradpour cases, significantly affect the curvature profile, structural complexity, equilibrium behavior, and ringdown response of traversable wormholes in modified $f(R,T)$ gravity.
