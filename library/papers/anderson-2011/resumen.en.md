The authors study how metamaterials could represent curved spacetimes, using the formal analogy between light in curved vacuum and light in a nonhomogeneous bianisotropic medium called a Tamm medium. They build a Tamm medium formulation, asymptotically equal to vacuum, for two cases: a spinning cosmic string and the Alcubierre drive. Using geometric optics they compute ray trajectories. For the Alcubierre drive, rays depend strongly on the bubble's speed and direction but little on the thickness of its wall, and the bubble acts as a focusing lens for rays travelling in the same direction, especially at high speed.
