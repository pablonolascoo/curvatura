The work examines whether an isolated concave object can feel a net force from a background of randomly created waves carrying momentum. It studies this first with waves on a liquid surface and then with quantum vacuum electromagnetic waves, in a rectangular structure open on one side whose interior holds many parallel reflecting plates. Using known results on the Casimir effect and Casimir-like effects, the authors explain why such a structure should feel net oriented forces. They also describe real systems with which it could be tested. It is of interest for vacuum-based propulsion.
