Carbon-made surfaces of constant negative Gaussian curvature realize portions of the Lobachevsky plane in the laboratory, with motivations ranging from analogue gravity, where the elliptic, Beltrami and hyperbolic pseudospheres correspond to the three static phases of the Bañados--Teitelboim--Zanelli black hole, to water desalination. We review and extend their construction around a single principle, the balanced Gauss--Bonnet theorem: the curvature fixed by the topology is shared among disclination defects, elastically deformed hexagons and boundaries. Curvature without defects cannot change the topology: a sphere made of hexagons only is a punctured sphere, as an explicit polyhedral construction shows. The arrangements of the defects on the three pseudospheres stem from the elliptic, parabolic and hyperbolic one-parameter subgroups of $SL(2,\mathbb{R})$: we construct them for the elliptic family, recover the known one of the Beltrami pseudosphere, and propose one for the hyperbolic pseudosphere. We compare these ideal arrangements with carbon structures obtained by design and by simulated nano-extrusion, and suggest that the infinitely many Fuchsian groups may correspond to the infinitely many Lobachevsky surfaces.
