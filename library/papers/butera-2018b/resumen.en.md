Phonons in a Bose-Einstein condensate behave as if they propagate in curved spacetime when the flow speed is controlled. The paper links this to artificial gauge potentials, induced by coupling atomic levels with a laser, and shows that density dependent synthetic gauge fields give phonons a nontrivial spacetime structure. This lets one design new geometries by tuning the transverse laser phase, while horizons still depend only on the flow speed. As an application, they simulate effectively charged phonons in 2+1 dimensions that move under a gravitational and an electromagnetic field at once.
