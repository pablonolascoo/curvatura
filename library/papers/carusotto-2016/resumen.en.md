This Nature Physics commentary presents experiments on an event horizon analogous to a black hole, but for sound waves in a quantum fluid. According to the available source, the results give strong evidence for a sonic analogue of Hawking radiation and are described as a milestone for quantum hydrodynamics. The underlying idea is that analogue gravity systems let researchers study horizon effects in the laboratory that are out of reach with real black holes. It is useful context for the quantum physics of horizons, which also arises in analyses of warp drives.
