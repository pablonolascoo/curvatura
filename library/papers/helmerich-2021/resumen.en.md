The authors tackle the optimization of warp drive geometries numerically, instead of evaluating analytic solutions of Einstein's equations. For a given metric they numerically compute the stress-energy-momentum tensor and from it obtain a fitness parameter measuring how efficiently a given warp velocity is achieved. The metric is then updated according to that parameter, with no analytic formula needed, which allows a much wider range of optimization techniques. The method also gives rarely discussed information about the distribution of energy density, momentum, shear stress and pressure across the warp bubble.
