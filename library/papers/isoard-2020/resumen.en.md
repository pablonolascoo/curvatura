This thesis studies nonlinear phenomena in two similar quantum fluids, Bose-Einstein condensates and fluids of light. The first part analyses sonic analogues of black holes, made with a condensate flowing from a subsonic to a supersonic region, where an analogue Hawking radiation emerges from vacuum fluctuations. It shows that including the zero modes, previously omitted in analogue gravity, is essential for an accurate description of the process and gives excellent agreement with experiments. It also finds that the system creates tripartite entanglement. The second part studies the propagation of nonlinear fluids with a hydrodynamic approach.
