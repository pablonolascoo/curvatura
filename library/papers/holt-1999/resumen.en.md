The paper examines how the International Space Station could be used to test materials and technologies tied to breakthrough propulsion physics. It builds on results such as non-local communication between light beams, the Casimir effect, possible weight reduction by a spinning superconductor, and extreme-power lasers reaching curvatures like those near a black hole horizon. It concludes that microgravity, extreme vacuum and plasma offer advantages for such tests. It is a feasibility study, not a report of experimental results.
