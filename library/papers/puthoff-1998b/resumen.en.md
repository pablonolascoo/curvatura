The paper reviews theory and experiments on whether the vacuum can be exploited for spaceflight applications. It recalls that quantum theory predicts, and experiments confirm, an enormous residual zero-point energy that influences phenomena such as the inhibition of spontaneous emission, the Casimir force and possibly sonoluminescence. For spaceflight it raises three lines: the origin and possible control of inertia, laboratory attempts to extract useful energy from vacuum fluctuations, and vacuum engineering, including whether warp drive is scientifically possible. The authors state that recent advances point to potential applications in all of them.
