This thesis studies how cinema presents the science of time travel to the public and whether this allows a comprehensive set of models to be built. The author identified models of time proposed by scientists and philosophers and determined the model of time used in each of 100 time travel films. Through surveys in three focus groups he also gathered the public's personal models. From all this he developed 21 models of time and diagrams of his own for analysing travellers' timelines. It is a science communication study, not a physics one.
