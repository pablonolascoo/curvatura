The authors ask whether the laws of physics forbid building closed timelike curves by manipulating matter and energy. They note that no widely accepted definition of a time machine exists and propose one: all extensions of the machine region must contain closed timelike curves. They review no-go theorems in classical general relativity, semiclassical quantum gravity, quantum field theory on curved spacetime and Euclidean quantum gravity. They conclude that no result is general enough to support a confident yes, and they highlight foundational problems at the junction of relativity and quantum physics.
