The work studies, theoretically and experimentally, the mutual interaction of self-focused optical filaments inside a ferrofluid-based hyperbolic metamaterial, whose nonlinear optics had already shown very unusual spatiotemporal dynamics. The authors observe that these interactions closely resemble general relativity in 2+1 dimensions, which predicts a predominantly topological, non-Newtonian character. This matters because 2+1 gravity is an exactly solvable theory, even in the quantum gravity limit. It is a laboratory analogue of gravitational effects, of indirect interest for spacetime physics.
