This book examines the reasons for exploring our neighboring stars and the technologies with which robotic probes able to cross interstellar distances could be built. Because the distances demand propulsion radically different from chemical rockets, the author reviews the various schemes proposed for interstellar travel, all based on current physics, and the challenges they pose to physicists, engineers and space entrepreneurs. He distinguishes methods with real physical grounding from pure fantasy, such as Star Trek's warp drive. It serves as a general introduction to deep space propulsion.
