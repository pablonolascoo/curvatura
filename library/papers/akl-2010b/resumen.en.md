The article assumes time travel is allowed and asks how much computing power a system with closed timelike curves would have. It shows that such a computer can solve four of the five problems recently put forward as counterexamples to the principle of universality in computation. The fifth counterexample remains unsolvable, so universality cannot be reached even with such an extraordinary aid. The link to propulsion is indirect: it deals with the computational consequences of closed timelike curves, which also appear in some wormhole and warp drive solutions.
