This bioethics article responds to Matti Häyry, who argues that space exploration aimed at human settlement is irrational and morally impermissible because it exposes others, including future generations, to risks they did not choose. The author rebuts the use of the precautionary principle as a decisive moral rule, because it treats abstention as ethically neutral and suits long term activities poorly. He argues that any moral failure lies not in exploring space but in mission frameworks that exclude normative, social and philosophical perspectives from design and governance. It does not deal with physics or propulsion.
