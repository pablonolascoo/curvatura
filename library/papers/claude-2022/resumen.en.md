This doctoral thesis studies polariton fluids, quasiparticles of light and matter in semiconductor microcavities that behave as quantum fluids. It develops a new high-resolution coherent spectroscopy method to measure collective excitations, the Bogoliubov modes. It examines how the driven-dissipative nature of the system affects the dynamics and its link to bistability, and observes modes that precede instabilities and a ghost branch. It also covers nonequilibrium phase transitions, a Goldstone mode, dark solitons and quantum turbulence. It relates to the analogue physics of quantum fluids.
