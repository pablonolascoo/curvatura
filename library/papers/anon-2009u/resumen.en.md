This review summarizes the nonlinear optical properties of a ruby crystal under weak light and their role in controlling group velocity. Ruby is treated as a two-level system that shows coherent population oscillations under laser pumping. Self-phase modulation also appears and produces Fraunhofer diffraction patterns in the far field, along with an effect similar to two-wave coupling. The competition among these three mechanisms can produce a self-acceleration of the optical group velocity. It is relevant as an experimental example of apparent superluminal propagation in material media.
