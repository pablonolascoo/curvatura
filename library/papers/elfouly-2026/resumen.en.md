The study formulates a deterministic finite-memory extension of Newtonian mechanics in which every interaction depends only on the realized past within explicit memory horizons. It argues that two phenomenological organizations emerge from one law: quantum-type behaviour in push-pull interaction channels and relativistic-type bookkeeping in attraction-only cases. Numerical simulations show interference-like fringes in a double slit and tunnelling-like transmission trends that depend on barrier shape. In the relativistic regime it obtains an operational time dilation and a derived Lorentz-type symmetry.
