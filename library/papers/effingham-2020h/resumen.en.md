This philosophy chapter analyzes the bootstrapping paradox: if time travel is possible, entities that partly cause their own existence would be possible, such as information brought from the future to build the time machine; yet such entities seem impossible. It distinguishes objects, information and causal loops. It argues that the paradox is avoided by denying that time travel requires bootstrapped objects and that there are no good reasons to think such entities impossible. It is a conceptual analysis, related to time machines.
