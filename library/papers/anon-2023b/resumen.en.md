Starting from the Akama-Diakonov theory of emergent tetrads, which allows two Planck constants with dimensions of time and length, the author studies what they mean in a system where the microscopic physics is known: phonons in a superfluid Bose liquid. There the acoustic metric defines acoustic Planck constants, and the length one turns out to be of the order of the interatomic distance. This supports the idea that the length constant is related to the Planck length. The acoustic metric is also used to discuss its dependence on the Hubble parameter in an expanding universe.
