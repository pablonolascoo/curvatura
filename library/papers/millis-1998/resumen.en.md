The paper presents preliminary results of an August 1997 NASA workshop that assessed what physics might offer for breakthroughs in space transport: propulsion needing no propellant mass, the maximum possible speeds and new ways to produce energy. A special emphasis was on identifying affordable, credible near-term research. Experiments and theories were discussed on the coupling of gravity and electromagnetism, vacuum fluctuation energy, warp drives, wormholes and superluminal quantum tunneling. It also reports the status of the propulsion program.
