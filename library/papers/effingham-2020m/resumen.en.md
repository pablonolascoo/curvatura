The author argues that time travellers might have the ability to do the metaphysically impossible, such as killing their grandfather, even though they never will. This requires physical possibility to outstrip metaphysical possibility, and he argues that this assumption is reasonable given the dialectic of the grandfather paradox. From it he derives what he calls impossibility theory. He sets out Jack Spencer's argument for the same conclusion and applies the idea to other paradoxes unrelated to time travel.
