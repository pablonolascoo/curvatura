The chapter starts from a philosophical paradox: time travel is possible and would let you change the past, yet changing the past is impossible. The author proposes two ways out. The first, called Ludovician, accepts that the past cannot be changed and denies that time travel requires changing it; it is argued to be incompatible with an open future. The second introduces indexed worlds, with extra universes or temporal dimensions, where the past can be changed. The metaphysical possibility of both is defended. It is a philosophy of time text.
