The work shows that the Einstein-Straus model does not give a robust answer to how much cosmic expansion influences local physics. It finds the most general static region that can be embedded in an expanding Friedmann-Lemaître-Robertson-Walker cosmology and proves the model must be almost spherically symmetric: the boundary of the static region must be a 2-sphere at each cosmic instant. The would-be centre of that sphere moves along a geodesic of the induced 3-metric, with velocity fixed by the matching. With standard energy-momentum tensors inside, that centre must be at rest relative to the cosmological flow.
