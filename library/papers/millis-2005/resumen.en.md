The article assesses possible propulsion breakthroughs, such as propellantless space drives and faster-than-light travel, that would make interstellar exploration practical. It reports the experience of NASA's Breakthrough Propulsion Physics project (1996-2002) in three facets: predicting benefits, selecting research and recent technical progress. Since the concepts are still hypothetical, it uses energy for comparison and notes that a hypothetical space drive would need many orders of magnitude less energy than a rocket to reach the nearest star. It describes a selection process focused on immediate unknowns and on the credibility of assertions.
