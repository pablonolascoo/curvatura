This thesis studies quantum light-matter interactions from three angles: how to use them to create exotic energy distributions, how well the classical light-matter approximation holds in the quantum case, and what causal consequences common approximations have in relativistic theories. For the first, it proposes a quantum energy teleportation protocol with stationary qubits that produces local negative energy densities without relativistic mirrors. Total negative energy grows as the inverse cube of the well width, with equally large positive peaks on either side, and this scaling saturates the quantum interest conjecture. It also examines the validity of the dipole model with a quantum electromagnetic field.
