This philosophy chapter analyses the tourist paradox: the absence of time travellers from the future seems to show that time travel is physically impossible. After introducing the paradox and reviewing existing solutions, the author argues that, given Ludovicianism, we should never expect to see time travellers, however high the objective chance that someone travels in time. He concludes that we would be rationally obliged to avoid experiments that bring about time travel, even accidentally, and sketches what this would mean for current high-energy experiments.
