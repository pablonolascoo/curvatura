This philosophy of science article examines what analogue experiments can teach, such as the dumb holes in fluids and Bose-Einstein condensates that mimic black holes. It compares these experiments with other cases of experiment and simulation in physics. The authors argue, against recent positions, that they cannot confirm the existence of phenomena in inaccessible systems, such as Hawking radiation, because they must assume the modelling framework is adequate for the target system, which makes the argument circular. It is relevant for judging the value of evidence from gravity analogues.
