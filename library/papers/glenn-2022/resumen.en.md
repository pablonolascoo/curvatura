The author argues mathematically that the energy density needed for Alcubierre's warp field can be positive if the shaping function is complex and its imaginary part exceeds the real part. He proposes an experiment with a material whose dielectric constant has a larger imaginary than real component, placed in an RF-driven resonant cavity, which would reproduce that shaping function. The work is presented as a starting structure for better experiments and gives no confirmed measurement. It bears on the negative-energy problem of warp drives.
