This philosophical chapter examines the time discrepancy paradox: if time travel were possible, in five minutes one could be in the year 1930, which looks like a contradiction. The standard reply separates external time from personal time, and the chapter explains it in full. It then explores the ontological and ideological commitments of that distinction. It ends by arguing that temporal relations can be reduced to immanent causal relations. It is a work in the metaphysics of time, useful as conceptual context for closed timelike curves.
