The work analyzes quantum effects in superluminal warp drives, which already would require a huge amount of exotic matter. It explains that quantum fields propagating in these geometries make them semiclassically unstable: a high-temperature Hawking-like particle flux is generated inside the bubble, causing exponential growth of the energy density at the front wall as seen by freely falling observers. The instability persists even when Lorentz symmetry is broken by higher-order terms. With subluminal dispersion a black-hole laser effect gives exponential amplification, and with superluminal dispersion the flux grows linearly.
