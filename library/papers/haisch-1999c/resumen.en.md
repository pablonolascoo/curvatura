The authors argue that an interstellar mission, though remote, is now a legitimate scientific topic, and that one route is to look for new regimes and laws of physics. They focus on the zero point field, or electromagnetic quantum vacuum, and the idea that the inertia of matter arises partly from the interaction of quarks and electrons with it. They report a new result from a NASA funded study: for the electron, a resonance of the inertia generating process at the Compton frequency would explain both its inertial mass and the de Broglie wavelength measured by Davisson and Germer. This suggests links among electrodynamics, inertia, gravitation and the wave nature of matter.
