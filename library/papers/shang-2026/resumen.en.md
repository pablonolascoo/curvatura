This review traces the evolution of de Broglie-Bohm theory, proposed in 1927 and now a complement to the orthodox interpretation of quantum mechanics. It is a nonlocal and contextual hidden-variable theory, compatible with Bell's inequality and the Kochen-Specker theorem. It summarizes recent experiments, from the 2011 reconstruction of average Bohmian trajectories by weak measurement to the 2016 and 2019 tests of their nonlocality. It focuses on nonlocal energy alteration, observed on a quantum memory platform stemming from the ESSW thought experiment, and includes research prospects. Its interest for propulsion lies in the question of nonlocality.
