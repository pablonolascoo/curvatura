Analogue models of black holes usually have collective excitations whose dispersion relation breaks the effective Lorentz symmetry at high energy. The work studies how this violation affects the quasinormal modes, the modes of energy dissipation. The model uses low frequency capillary-gravity waves around a draining vortex, which mimics a rotating black hole, with dispersion adjustable between subluminal and superluminal modifications. With a subluminal or superluminal modification, the frequency and decay rate of corotating modes can drop or rise substantially, while counterrotating modes barely change. With the superluminal one, for strong violations critical rotations appear below or above which some modes do not exist.
