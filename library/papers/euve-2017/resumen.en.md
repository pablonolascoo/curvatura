This thesis aims to observe Hawking radiation in the laboratory, the prediction that black holes emit radiation. Because it is too faint and distant in real objects, it follows Unruh's proposal of using hydrodynamic systems that obey the same equations. Specifically, it exploits the correspondence between light near a black hole and surface waves on a counter-current made inhomogeneous by a submerged obstacle. This requires a deep understanding of surface wave mechanics, including variable bathymetry, vorticity and nonlinearities, and a free-surface measurement method was developed and optimized. It is an analogue gravity work.
