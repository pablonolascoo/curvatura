The authors use the need to avoid the trans-Planckian problem of Hawking radiation as a guiding principle for finding convincing scenarios of black hole, or black hole like object, evaporation. They argue that only three scenarios are possible, depending on whether high energy physics keeps the classical long lived horizon and on whether the observed dark compact objects have such a horizon at all. Among the findings, a theory with high energy superluminal signalling and a long lived trapping horizon would be extremely unstable astrophysically. Also, pulsations of objects hovering extremely close to their gravitational radius could produce Hawking like emission.
