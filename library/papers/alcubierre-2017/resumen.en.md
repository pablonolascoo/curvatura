This chapter introduces the basics of warp drive spacetimes, which serve as thought experiments for probing the foundations of general relativity and for defining precisely what faster-than-light travel and communication mean. It reviews their definition and properties, notably the violation of the energy conditions, the appearance of horizons in the superluminal case, and the possibility of building closed timelike curves. Because of the horizon problem, an observer aboard the ship cannot create or control the bubble on demand, so the text discusses Krasnikov's metric, in which the round trip time measured at the starting point can be made arbitrarily short.
