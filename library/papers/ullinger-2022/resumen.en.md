An event horizon produces a logarithmic phase singularity in the mode functions of a massless scalar field, characteristic of Hawking radiation. The authors show that similar features are present in an elementary quantum system, the inverted harmonic oscillator. They analyze its energy eigenfunctions and phase-space representation, where the horizon and the logarithmic phase are hidden and are revealed by a coordinate transformation. The Fourier transform of the logarithmic phase gives an expression reminiscent of a specific quantum statistics, which governs the oscillator's reflection and transmission coefficients.
