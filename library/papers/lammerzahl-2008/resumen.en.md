The article explains why gravity experiments are so exciting. It recalls that gravity, being geometry, allows black holes, nontrivial topologies, time travel, warp drives and other phenomena foreign to non-relativistic physics. It presents the experimental basis of general relativity, especially the Einstein equivalence principle and its predictions in the weak and strong gravity regimes. It discusses routes to search for signals of a quantum theory of gravity and highlights rarely discussed assumptions, such as Newton's axioms or conservation laws. It also proposes an experiment testing the order of time derivatives in the equation of motion.
