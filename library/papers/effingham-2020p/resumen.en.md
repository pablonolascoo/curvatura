The chapter examines how probability would work in time travel scenarios, both for the Ludovician and for those who believe in indexed worlds. In the former case, metaphysical impossibilities can have positive objective chance, though our credence in them should be zero. The upshot is that we should never expect time travel to happen: the more feasible a time machine looks, the more likely some calamity prevents it. At an indexed world, credence and chance do not come apart, and time travel might be expected. It is philosophy of science.
