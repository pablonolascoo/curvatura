In a one-dimensional Bose gas there is no non-trivial scattering channel of three Bogoliubov quasiparticles that conserves both energy and momentum. Even so, the authors show that these three-wave mixing processes, Beliaev and Landau damping, account for the decay of phonons through interaction with thermal fluctuations. In the time window where Fermi's golden rule applies, the occupation of the initial mode decays exponentially with a simple analytic rate, which agrees well with truncated Wigner approximation simulations. These processes also slow down the growth of phonons from parametric oscillation.
