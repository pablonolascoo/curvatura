This history thesis studies, through the lens of power, projects for exploring and occupying interstellar space in situ during the twentieth and twenty-first centuries. It shows how space went from being observed to being desired and necessary, and how a varied research field, interstellar studies, emerged. It asks whether this rise is complete or held back by limits, and how the interstellar option raises questions of power and reorganizes human relations. It distinguishes near and far space, with different challenges. It is a work of history and political science rather than physics.
