This chapter reviews the experimental basis of general relativity and asks whether the postulates on dynamics and gravitation have empirical support. It starts from the idea that gravity is geometry and therefore allows horizons, black holes, nontrivial topologies, the big bang, time travel and warp drives. It examines the foundations, encoded in Einstein's equivalence principle, and their predictions in weak and strong gravity regimes, and discusses approaches to detecting the influence of a quantum theory of gravity. It reviews how far Newton's axioms and conservation laws are tested and examines higher order time derivatives.
