This chapter addresses the ethical governance of a worldship, a slower-than-light starship whose voyage lasts centuries and on which several generations live and die aboard. It argues that any governing institution should maximize recognition of the crew's human rights. It examines lifelong confinement and the reproductive limits imposed by a closed, sustainable environment, suggesting large crews and ships or advanced technology as ways out. It claims the worldship is probably our most realistic route to the stars and an extreme ethical test case. The approach is ethical rather than physical.
