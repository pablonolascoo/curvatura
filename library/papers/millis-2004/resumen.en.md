The article reviews the prospects for breakthrough propulsion from physics, with concepts such as space drives, warp drives and wormholes that are now discussed in reputable journals. It summarises NASA's Breakthrough Propulsion Physics Project, active from 1996 to 2002, with three grand challenges: propellantless propulsion, hyper-fast travel and new ways to power spacecraft. Because these challenges are far from fruition, modest, affordable research was favoured. Of 16 completed tasks, about a third proved not viable, a quarter have clear follow-ups and the rest remain unresolved.
