The work examines laser light propagating through thermo-optical defocusing media, described hydrodynamically as a quantum fluid of interacting photons, as a room-temperature alternative to atomic or exciton-polariton condensates for studying many-body physics. With a technique taken from oceanography it directly measures the single-particle part of the dispersion relation of the excitations and detects the global flow of the fluid. With a pump-and-probe setup it studies the dispersion of the modes and observes, for very long wavelengths, a dispersionless sonic propagation that it interprets as a signature of superfluidity. It is useful as an analogue for analogue gravity.
