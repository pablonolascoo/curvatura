This chapter in the philosophy of time treats the self-visitation paradox: if time travel is possible, one could visit oneself and have incompatible properties, such as two different heights or weights. The author argues this is not impossible and that time travelers simply end up with incompatible properties, with no need for sophisticated metaphysical theories. He closes by asking whether this possibility tells us something about ontology, for instance that perdurantism is true, and answers that it does not. It is a philosophical analysis with no physical calculations.
