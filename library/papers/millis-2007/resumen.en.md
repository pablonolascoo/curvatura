The work compares the energy requirements of hypothetical propellant-less space drives with those of rockets, to give initial estimates of benefit and suggest analytical approaches. It defines a space drive as an idealized propulsion that converts stored potential energy into kinetic energy using only the interaction between the spacecraft and the surrounding space. For Earth to orbit it would use 3.7 times less energy. For deep space, energy scales with the square of delta-v while rocket energy scales exponentially, giving 150 orders of magnitude advantage for a 5000 kg probe covering 5 light-years in 50 years. Indefinite levitation would need about 62 MJ/kg.
