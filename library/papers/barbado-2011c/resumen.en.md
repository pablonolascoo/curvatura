The authors use the avoidance of the trans-Planckian problem of Hawking radiation as a guiding principle to look for scenarios of black hole evaporation or black-hole-like objects. They argue that only three scenarios are possible, depending on whether high-energy physics preserves a long-lived horizon and whether the observed compact objects have one. They conclude that a theory with high-energy superluminal signalling and a long-lived trapping horizon would be highly unstable astrophysically. They also note that pulsations of objects hovering very near their gravitational radius could produce Hawking-like emission.
