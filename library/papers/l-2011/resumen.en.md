This article is a review of the state of computational aeroelasticity, which integrates computational fluid dynamics and computational structural dynamics and is increasingly used in aerospace design. It addresses two application areas, airframe and turbomachinery design, with an overview of the prediction methods for each. It discusses different levels of nonlinear modelling, the trade-off between accuracy and cost, order reduction techniques and future trends. It does not deal with exotic physics or advanced propulsion.
