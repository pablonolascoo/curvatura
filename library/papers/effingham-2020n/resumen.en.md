The chapter examines incapacity theory, which solves the grandfather paradox by saying that someone who travels to the past loses the ability to kill their grandfather. The author argues that this is David Lewis's position, based on how the words can and could are analysed, and does not think it is correct. He argues that taking the paradox and contemporary advances in modality seriously requires accepting impossible worlds and non-trivial counterpossibles. With them he reconstructs a variant of the paradox that the incapacity theorist cannot escape. It is a philosophical analysis of time travel.
