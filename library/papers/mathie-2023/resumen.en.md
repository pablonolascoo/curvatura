This philosophy of science chapter analyses the use of analogical reasoning in contemporary black hole physics. It describes two central analogical arguments: one supports the existence of astrophysical Hawking radiation and the other that black holes are genuinely thermodynamical. The author argues they are distinct but that the success of the second presupposes that of the first. This creates a tension for those who take black hole thermodynamics seriously but doubt the evidence from analogue gravity. She examines three ways out and concludes that none works.
