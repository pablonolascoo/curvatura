This is an extensive review of analogue gravity: the programme that reproduces relativistic gravitational fields in other physical systems, mostly condensed matter. The authors begin with a simple example and then go through the history, aims and results of the various analogue models. They note that the last decade has seen a large growth in publications and in collaboration between communities. They see good experimental and theoretical prospects, including inspiration for approaching quantum gravity. It provides a basis for studying analogues of black holes and exotic metrics in the laboratory.
