This perspective describes how a tabletop nonlinear optics experiment can reproduce the Penrose process, the mechanism by which energy is extracted from the rotation of a Kerr black hole. It combines laser propagation in a thermo-optic medium, viewed as a photon fluid, with rotating black hole geometries in a photon superfluid and the Zel'dovich effect. It also notes that the Penrose process has led to the idea of geometry-induced phase matching. It is a laboratory analogue of black hole physics.
