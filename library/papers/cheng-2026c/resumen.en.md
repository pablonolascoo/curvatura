The authors simulate the Unruh effect in the laboratory using a laser-controlled trapped ion that acts as an oscillating detector coupled to a cavity field. They observe joint excitation of the detector and the field in the detector's frame, consistent with what the Unruh effect predicts. They also simulate single and quantum-superposed trajectories, and in the latter case see coherent interference of the excitation. The result shows properties of superpositions of accelerated trajectories, linked to quantum gravity theories, that have no classical counterpart.
