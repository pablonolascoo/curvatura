This thesis studies classical and quantum scalar fields in inhomogeneous or time-dependent environments, in models described, fundamentally or effectively, as a curved spacetime with an event horizon. It shows how a mathematical correspondence, arising from an effective low-energy Lorentz symmetry, links wave behaviour in nonrelativistic systems to black hole physics, what its limits are and how far the analogue results reproduce the gravitational ones. After an introduction covering Hawking radiation, it details four of the author's own works.
