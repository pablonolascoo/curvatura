The paper studies sonic black holes in a one-dimensional relativistic flow. In a single spatial dimension, Unruh's analogy between sound in a fluid and light in curved spacetime generally fails. Considering a relativistic fluid, the author finds the general condition for an acoustic metric to exist in strictly one-dimensional systems. He examines the physical meaning of the equations of state and the symmetry of the basic equations. Finally he computes the Hawking temperature in an artificial de Laval nozzle. It is an analogue-gravity model.
