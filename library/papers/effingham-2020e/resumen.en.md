This chapter classifies the ways in which one might hypothetically travel to the past. Since no real case is known, the modes are drawn from fiction, historical thought and speculative physics. It considers a discontinuous jump into the past, like teleportation, and also passing continuously through the intervening instants. It also contemplates warping spacetime to return to the starting point, which is close to closed timelike curves. It ends with two connected cases that are not strictly time travel: frozen time and time as an illusion. The approach is philosophical, not a physical calculation.
