This article examines the problems of Alcubierre's warp drive, which on paper has no speed limit, leaves passengers weightless and avoids time dilation. In its original form it suffers serious flaws: unreasonably high energies, energy moving in a locally spacelike direction, and a violation of the energy conditions of classical relativity. The author presents a possible solution to one of these problems and suggests ways to at least soften the others. It is an early reference on the energetic viability of warp drives.
