This work reviews traversable wormholes, both static and dynamic, as solutions of Einstein's equations. It starts from faster-than-light effects and a historical overview, then focuses on the static spherically symmetric Morris-Thorne model. It discusses energy conditions in detail, their usual violations and attempts to quantify exotic matter. It then considers two dynamic families, evolving and rotating wormholes, deriving the solutions with computer algebra and Cartan calculus. It serves as an introduction to wormhole physics and to its reliance on negative energy.
