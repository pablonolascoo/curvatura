This chapter deals with constrict theories, in which a law of nature resolves the Grandfather Paradox. It introduces two positions: the chronology protection conjecture, whereby the laws rule out time travel, and Novikov's self-consistency principle, which allows it but prevents paradoxes. The author argues that, since the Grandfather Paradox is a thought experiment about metaphysical possibility, these theories are irrelevant to it. It ends by asking whether the paradox should be reread as an argument for physical impossibility.
