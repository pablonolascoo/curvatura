This is a metaphysics article, not a physics one. It examines universalism, the thesis that any objects always compose a further object, and three arguments from elegance often used in its favor. The author argues that a perdurantist cannot rely on them, because to avoid badly behaved superluminal objects they must answer the proper continuant question. That answer yields a restricted composition just as elegant as universalism, so the arguments from elegance do not motivate it in that case.
