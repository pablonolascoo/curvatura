The chapter presents the Schwinger-Keldysh (in-in) effective action, needed to derive the dynamics of expectation values. Unlike the in-out action, it yields a real and causal equation of motion, important for backreaction effects such as dissipation. It is built for quantum fields in curved spacetime, shown to need the same regularization, and applied to initial states described by a density matrix. There are two applications: damping of anisotropy in a Bianchi Type I universe and the stress-energy tensor of a Phi-4 field. It ends with thermal field theory.
