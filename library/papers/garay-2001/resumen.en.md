The authors review spacetimes containing nonchronal regions separated from causal regions by chronology horizons, that is, geometries with closed timelike curves. They investigate their connection with aspects expected in a final theory of quantum gravity. These include stability under classical and quantum metric fluctuations, boundary conditions of the universe and gravitational topological defects such as spacetime kinks. It matters because wormholes and some warp drives can create regions with closed timelike curves, and their quantum stability decides whether they are possible.
