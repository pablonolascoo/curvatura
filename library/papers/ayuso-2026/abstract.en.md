Wormhole solutions in General Relativity can only exist in the presence of exotic matter that violates the null energy condition. In this paper, we investigate the possibility of constructing wormhole solutions in $f(Q)$ gravity in which the extra connection degrees of freedom contribute such that no exotic matter is needed. Considering the Ellis-Bronnikov wormhole as an example, we find that the system admits two families of connections consistent with the spacetime symmetries and the requirements of symmetric teleparallel gravity. For both families, we show that constant non-metricity scalar $Q$ is not merely a simplifying assumption but arises naturally from the field equations when $f_{QQ}$ is regular, and that the resulting exact analytic solutions can satisfy the null energy condition throughout the entire spacetime. For the second family, which possesses richer connection degrees of freedom, we further demonstrate that solutions with a spatially varying $Q$ exist when $f_{QQ}$ is allowed to diverge at a finite value of $Q$, and that these too can satisfy the null energy condition everywhere. Our results show that the nontrivial connection degrees of freedom in $f(Q)$ gravity can naturally be responsible for supporting wormhole geometries, without resorting to any exotic matter.
