This chapter reviews faster-than-light approaches within general relativity, focusing on traversable wormholes and so-called stargates, a simple special case of such solutions. It recalls their origin in the 1985 work of Morris and Thorne and the later line of research on wormholes and time machines. It explains that travelers would cross the throat at local speed c, without violating special relativity, while an outside observer would see a superluminal crossing. It serves as an accessible introduction to propulsion through spacetime geometry.
