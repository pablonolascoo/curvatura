This review article traces black hole thermodynamics from the discovery in the early 1970s that black holes radiate like black bodies. It summarizes the many independent ways of obtaining their thermodynamic and possibly statistical properties, and presents them as hints about quantum gravity. It closes with the open problems: the nature of the quantum microstates, the universality of the results and the information loss paradox. It supplies the thermodynamic framework often used to constrain exotic physics.
