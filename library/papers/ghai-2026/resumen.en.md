This work integrates nonlinear optics, astrophysics and spectroscopic analysis to simulate black hole behaviour with a view to cloaking applications. It discusses energy-extraction mechanisms from relativistic jets and magnetic fields, such as Blandford-Znajek and Penrose, and laboratory analogues using Kerr-type refractive index changes that modify the effective radius and the Hawking-like radiation. It proposes that varying the probe position near an optical horizon changes the sensitivity, and that spectroscopy confirms the invisibility effect.
