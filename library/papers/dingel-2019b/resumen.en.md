The authors present an optical analogue of Einstein velocity addition, a central concept of special relativity underlying many relativistic effects. The analogy is built in a photonic circuit: the complex electric field at the output of an optical add-drop filter corresponds to relativistic velocity addition. They equate the two normalized velocities with the coupling coefficients of the filter's two directional couplers, and the generating angle with the single pass phase shift. They analyze its magnitude and direction angle in detail, toward the idea of special relativity on a chip.
